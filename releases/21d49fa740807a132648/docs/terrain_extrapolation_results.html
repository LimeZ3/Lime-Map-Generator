Terrain Control Extrapolation

The best 28.8M-parameter multiscale checkpoint was tested without retraining or game launches. Eighteen settings x three matched seeds = 54 finite samples. Sampling used 100 DDIM steps, the same requested base pair and initial noise per seed, default CFG 1.5, and the existing deterministic symmetry/water/smoothing decoder. No sea-level adjustment to the requested percentage, spawn repairs or resource placement were applied.

Training Support

Only the 1,020 training maps define these ranges, not validation examples.

  • Water label: 0-93.65%; only 16 maps at >=80%, none at >=95%.
  • Terrain relief (height p95-p05): 0.23-71.76 world units.
  • Typical total front width: 11.32-571.82 world units among 894 valid labels.
  • Sustained restriction length: 0-249.01 world units among valid labels.

An exact percentage not seen before, such as 12.3%, can still be interpolation inside the observed range. Requests of 95% and 100% water are true marginal extrapolation. Individual ranges do not prove that a combined profile has support; the saved configuration includes nearest standardized training-profile distances.

At a 100% request the three outputs range from 99.57% to 99.99% water. One retains two tiny dry base areas; two submerge both requested base positions. The ocean floor still has relief and depth. This is not an exact all-water guarantee or a playable-map certification. Exact all-water coverage cannot also contain dry land bases or land routes; the usual land-connected profile is contradictory at this endpoint, and requested land connectivity is not a hard constraint either.

Additional 100% probes:

  • Request no land link and mask width conditions: mean 98.95% water.
  • CFG 1: mean 98.02% water.
  • CFG 3: mean 98.72% water, with more seabed relief (27.29 versus 13.47 at CFG 1.5).

Increasing guidance is not a monotonic percentage-correction tool: all supplied conditions are amplified together and their effects are coupled. These results are exploratory, not a universal CFG ranking.

Other Extreme Controls

  • Relief 0 produces mean relief 4.62, not a perfectly flat map. Water rises to 36.11% despite a fixed 20% request, and the terrain becomes fragmented. Mixed land/water and the coastal decoder also prevent treating zero relief as a simple independent knob.
  • Relief 100 produces mean 148.30; relief 200 produces 209.93. Both are beyond the training relief range and create dramatic terrain. One 100 case blocks a base; one 200 case disconnects the bases. Above-water does not mean passable/buildable.
  • Front-width requests 50 versus 650 produce mean widths about 319 versus 437, far from the requested absolute targets. Linked span/bottleneck widths scale consistently with the requested front, so this is not a one-coordinate causal test.
  • Contraction 0.995 plus restriction length 300 does not produce a sustained narrow bottleneck: final measured restriction length is zero in all three seeds.

The seed-303 flat-terrain sample exceeds the geometry teacher's cross-section safety bound for its final terrain. The tool records that measurement as unavailable, not zero, and does not silently coarsen it. Its PNG and basic height/water metrics remain available. All other requested measurements completed.

Artifacts

data/models/terrain_extrapolation_v1/index.html contains five PNG comparison panels, measured averages and a response curve. config.json stores requests, support diagnostics and checkpoint identity; results.json contains raw/final measurements, exact coverage, depth and base-above-water checks. arrays/ retains encoded predictions and final native vertices. No geometry originality audit was run for these extreme samples; familiar-looking features are not evidence of a new unique map or a copy by themselves.

Main conclusion: the model can generate sensible-looking outputs beyond observed water/relief values, but controls are learned preferences, not exact specifications. Hard numeric requirements belong in a decoder/validator that also resolves their conflicts with playable-base requirements.
