Five-minute economic pull with mex construction

The fixed test now evaluates all11 pilot maps and their8 existing contact budgets. It retains the approved1s per-entity acquisition and three map-relative tiers. It does not rerun route finding or replace the saved route-fit weights. The viewer labels those old weights explicitly and adds a separate Five-minute economic pull panel with ranked route contributions and an engineer timeline.

One shared work and financing schedule

The existing eligible own-side land-front pool is reused, including a separately labelled Base-preferred allocation. Protected starting/rear resources used only for map-level tier calibration do not enter this pool. No resource or engineer capacity is created when the contact budget increases.

The300s window is shared by building, reclaiming and any financing waits. A36-mass working-capital advance permits the first mex and is explicitly excluded from economic gain. Mass is reserved/paid before construction; this conservative cash model does not simulate the game's continuous construction resource drain. There is no assumed pre-existing starting-base income in this frontier ledger. Existing completed mexes generate income while the engineer works or waits.

At each step, choose the feasible task with the highest incremental net horizon mass return per occupied engineer-second:

  • Mex: income after its actual completion minus construction mass, divided by build time plus any required wait for financing. Negative-return late builds are skipped.
  • Reclaim: attainable mass divided by mass/25 + 1, using the reviewed hypothesis. A final partial object pays acquisition once and yields only the mass processed before the horizon. No reward is credited for an unfinished acquisition.

This is a feasible greedy schedule, not a proof of the optimal finite-horizon strategy. A complete reclaim task is credited on completion; stream financing within a reclaim operation is not simulated. The tiers describe its stock and work without adding3:2:1 multipliers.

Equal-value tasks are interchangeable in a no-travel comparison. Their route ownership is averaged analytically. For example, building one of two equivalent mexes on symmetric paths gives each path half the expected credit. Physical jobs remain serial and completed; fractional route counts are expected attribution, not fractional constructions or parallel engineers. Existing tied ownership is also conserved. This prevents arbitrary task-ID ordering from inventing asymmetric path importance.

For every pool:

net mass = mex production since completion - mex construction mass + reclaimed mass

Shared totals, per-tier collected mass/work and initial/final cash are exported. Mex-only and reclaim-only isolation controls each have the same300s budget. They cannot be added to claim the income of one engineer. The previous instant-mex + independent reclaim estimate is retained as a clearly labelled comparison.

Energy accounting and limits

Construction energy and upkeep after completion are charged to a separate ledger. Known energy recovered from scheduled mass-bearing props/wrecks is credited from the same physical operation, without charging collection work again. Unknown companion values remain unknown and are counted. Energy-only props stay in the inventory but are not selected by this mass-focused schedule.

The report exposes gross energy spending, known reclaimed energy, final net demand and peak cumulative deficit. Construction energy is reserved upfront in this ledger, not a prediction of instantaneous drain. No power-generation construction, energy stalls, storage/overflow or energy-to-mass exchange rate is simulated. Rankings are therefore power-supplied mass opportunities with an energy bill, not energy-feasible match-income predictions. The energy stage can later compete for this same work budget and use a matching joint-resource processing-time model.

Travel, mex upgrades, adjacency, enemy denial and additional engineer production are also outside this first test. Current path geometry stays unchanged, allowing the economic interpretation to be reviewed independently.
