Corridor-cut implementation

Implemented 14 October 2026. The user's proposed midpoint-cut search replaces waypoint sampling in this new pilot; historical previews remain available. Scope is Adaptive Archsimkats Valley, slots 1 to 2, using cached land/amphibious/ hover masks. It performs no new naval reach calculation or library-wide run.

Follow-up: see docs/route_areas_and_widths.md for width profiles, walking-distance territories, shared sections/resources and the rotation diagnostic for routes 6/7. These enrich the same route geometry.

Algorithm

  1. Expand shortest travel distance through the current grid with compiled weighted Dijkstra. This gives the same shortest-distance objective as two correctly terminated weighted waves; it does not literally simulate two circular fronts. Straight moves cost 1, diagonals sqrt(2), with both adjoining side cells required for a diagonal.
  2. Locate the route midpoint by travelled distance. Estimate its local tangent from nearby route points. Find nearest original terrain-boundary cells in each left/right half-plane, including the map edge as a boundary.
  3. Rasterize a cross-section obstacle→midpoint→obstacle. This is two line segments when the nearest endpoints are not collinear; it guarantees the cut crosses the route. Make its cells temporarily impassable and search again. Later cuts use the subproblem's original boundary, not earlier temporary cuts.
  4. Check a cut on both the modified grid and the original subproblem. A cut that closes only the remaining connection means the available corridors have been exhausted. It is not automatically a shared chokepoint.
  5. If the cut also disconnects the original subproblem, look for a genuinely narrow separator along the route. Use 19 samples between 15% and 85% of route length. A shared choke must be no wider than 24 map units and at most 65% of the widest sampled section. A wide separator through a uniform open field does not become a fake choke.
  6. Create an intermediate node at a real passable cell of the choke. Restrict the two subproblem masks to their respective components plus the restored original passable cut cells. Discover alternatives on both sides and form route combinations through the node. Record each segment as a graph edge.
  7. Merge repeated terrain-side signatures and crossing choices. Retain the inherited significant-barrier thresholds (area 128 cells, or area 16 with span 32) to avoid reporting tiny terrain variations as strategic corridors. Validate every full route against the untouched original movement mask.

The graph records spawn and shared-chokepoint nodes and segment edges. This version does not yet split every overlapping near-spawn stretch into junctions. The chosen intermediate cell approximates an entire gate; gate-wide entrance optimization and complete corridor graph topology remain future refinements.

Matching means identical significant-barrier and new-passage signatures, not identical cell polylines. Shortest-path tie choices and temporary cuts can change the exact geometry. Amphibious and hover each yield seven families: the five land-style options plus two central crossing choices. No search budget/depth/ stretch limit was reached on this pilot. All three layers take about four seconds including registered PNG output on this machine.

The viewer exposes each temporary cut and its decision, graph nodes, route-to- edge sequences, previous route IDs, distance, detour and clearance. The separate synthetic shared-gate fixture has two branching regions joined by one neck. It produces one intermediate node, four segment edges and all four 2×2 combinations. This verifies that shared bottlenecks do not delete alternatives beyond them. The interactive synthetic demonstration is available at http://127.0.0.1:5177/navigation_corridors/shared_gate_demo/index.html.

Validation and limits

Twenty-one navigation/route tests pass, including open-ground grouping, both sides of a barrier, movement-specific water access, no diagonal corner cutting, source-mask immutability, shared-gate combinations and explicit depth limits.

The algorithm is a bounded greedy prototype, not exhaustive route enumeration or a guarantee of army clearance. Default guards: 20 cuts per segment, depth 6, 64 subproblems, 24 combinations, 2.5× length and 24-unit minimum segment length. Truncation reasons appear in JSON. Terrain masks preserve the existing 0.75 height difference and amphibious maximum depth of 25. Props, unit footprints, dynamic obstructions and engine steering are not modeled. Nearest-side width is a geometric cross-section estimate, not validated usable corridor width.
