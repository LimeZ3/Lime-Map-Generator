The community navigation uses 0.75 as its corner height-difference threshold and 25 as maximum amphibious depth, consistent with our chosen numerical limits and current FAF navigation. The meaning of the slope test differs: our gradient is calculated after averaging vertices into cells. Their test checks all four edges, and uses strict < 0.75 as FAF does.

A synthetic dry 64×64-cell map with a 0.75-high step demonstrates the difference: our current mask marks all 4,096 cells passable; the FAF corner rule blocks the 64 cells along the cliff. The arithmetic check is saved in data/raw/community_research/synthetic_pathing_check.json. This checks the corner rule only; the complete C# runtime has not been benchmarked here.

Limitations that still need work

  1. AI navigation is not complete physical movement. The port documents deviations in label IDs, areas and some isolated labels. Its shortest routes run on grid cells, whereas FAF's NavUtils runs between quadtree leaves. Small resource-free regions are deliberately removed for AI purposes. Keep a physical terrain mask and AI navigation labels as separate data layers. Unit footprints, props, wrecks, dynamic obstructions and actual engine movement still need independent validation.
  2. Resource roles are heuristics. Defaults are a 60-unit base radius, 15% distance margin for contestability, 25-unit grouping spacing and groups of three for expansions. They are useful continuous distances plus adjustable classifications, not authoritative safety labels. The algorithm compares starts, not enemy teams. Team setups need enemy/allied ownership, and spots with straight-line fallback must retain their Routed=false status.
  3. Static files do not resolve runtime adaptation. The loader reads scenario, save and terrain, not the map's gameplay script. Our supported default/slot adapters remain useful. Broader adaptive option execution stays outside MVP.
  4. Statistics need matching definitions. Their water share samples one vertex per cell over the full map; ours averages corners over playable bounds. Differences near shorelines/cropped margins need definitions before using one tool as an oracle. The stored enabled-water flag is handled explicitly.
  5. Legacy coverage matters. Their parser supports 53/56/60. Our successfully processed library includes 26 format-52 maps. A wholesale parser replacement would lose that coverage unless extended.

Annotation feedback

Separate measurements, estimates and human judgements:

Topic Proposed treatment
Gameplay quality/fun Do not use an unconditioned universal score as a generation target; preserve old feedback separately
Visual appeal Keep human assessment; show representative visuals, and record the assessed game/setup context
Terrain readability Keep human assessment: does appearance correctly communicate routes, depth and usable terrain?
Spawn-imposed specialization Keep for team-map scope; on a symmetric 1v1 subset it has little variation and is usually irrelevant
Land/navy access, openness, expansion, build space Calculate route/access/width/contiguous-area features; validate their interpretation on known maps
Air importance, T1/T2/T3 importance, drops, eco safety Derive explainable proxies, but do not present them as deterministic outcomes of terrain alone

Examples: resource arrival times depend on route distance and unit speed; drop opportunity can include land-detour distance and reachable landing space; base space requires contiguous buildable footprints, not total flat percentage; naval usefulness requires water connections, launch space and useful coastlines; economic exposure requires enemy access and resource/reclaim placement. Faction, unit ranges/costs, tech timings, match settings and balance revision affect these estimates. All-air passability alone does not measure air importance.

Beauty is also subjective, but it directly assesses intended appearance. A model that only generates height/resource tensors cannot learn every texture/lighting property from a beauty score. Initially use visual ratings for curation and evaluate the actual rendered outputs; texture/prop-style generation can follow.

Proposed first target

The following 10×10/1v1 suggestion has been superseded by the user's explicit featured-map scope. See strategic_map_features_plan.md and the frozen 33-map manifest from 14 October 2026 for the current plan.

10×10 km, ordinary 1v1, fixed/default resource configuration, land-connected spawns, with ladder examples as a curated reference set. Keep water features where compatible; do not start with team-role, scripted-survival or all-naval generation. Two slots is an uncomplicated initial restriction; some valid 1v1 ladder setups use maps with more available slots and can be added explicitly.

Our 8 October 2026 snapshot contains 299 visible, ranked 512×512 maps with exactly two available slots; 297 already have overlays. This is a candidate cohort, not a count of current ladder maps. Ranked means rating eligibility, not ladder membership or guaranteed design quality. Family deduplication, active-pair validation and land connectivity will narrow the set further. These counts do not prove that the cohort is large enough to train a strong diffusion model from scratch.

Maintain the full archive, but concentrate annotation, correctness checks and the first generator evaluation on the subset. Split training/evaluation by map family so variants/rotations do not leak across splits. Broader self-supervised pretraining can use compatible unlabeled maps, with the first generator's output and evaluation scope still restricted.

Suggested next milestone

  1. Pin and attribute the community code. Prefer a small C# command-line adapter around its existing map/navigation libraries emitting machine-readable results for local files; our Python pipeline can keep tensors and PNGs.
  2. Compare a small validation pack before any library-wide replacement: dry flat terrain, threshold cliffs, blocked terrain types, shoreline cases, steep underwater terrain, depth 25, cropped playable areas and narrow gaps. Include Dragons Tooth Mountains, Setons, DualGap, Canis and a few 1v1 ladder examples. Validate boundary disagreements in-game, not just visually.
  3. Export separate land/hover/amphibious/naval region labels and route-distance features. Keep existing images and versions until the new masks are accepted.
  4. Define a few human-readable 1v1 metrics (rush route, expansion access, reclaim access, usable base space, route alternatives); retain raw values and versioned thresholds. Keep human annotation focused on appearance/readability.
  5. Evaluate a narrow generator with constraints for valid spawns, reachable resources and build space, plus human inspection of appearance/readability.
