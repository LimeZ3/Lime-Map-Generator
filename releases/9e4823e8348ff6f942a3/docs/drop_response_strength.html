Route and base response strength

15 October 2026. Viewer: http://127.0.0.1:5177/drop_response_strength_1v1/index.html

This extends the calibrated drop model with independent normalized land-route and base ground-response strengths. It reuses the three maps' 1v1 geometry, resources, physical travel times and economic presets. No extraction, route search or naval sweep is repeated.

Meaning

Strength 1 is the previous full-strength response. Strength 0.5 or 0.25 models reduced suppressive capacity; strength 0 means that source has no suppressive effect. The strength is a scenario assumption about available/effective defenders, not movement speed, a unit count or terrain-derived gameplay importance.

A force may arrive immediately yet be too weak to stop the drop. To represent this without inventing a slower walking time, the model retains physical ETA t and computes an opportunity window in seconds-equivalent over assessment horizon H = 60:

effective_window(t, strength) = H - strength * (H - min(t, H))
combined_window = min(route_window, base_window)

At strength 1, this is the original response window capped at 60, which preserves every baseline candidate flag and destination count. At strength 0.25, a route with zero walking ETA gives a 45-second-equivalent opportunity score; that is not a prediction of 45 real seconds without resistance. A nearby full-strength base can still veto the location. The two response sources are not summed; combined weaker armies are outside this approximation.

The existing strict/medium/permissive effective-window thresholds remain 35/22/12 seconds-equivalent. The weighted window also drives the existing capped income-denial ceiling. Expansion resource value, production cost, physical arrival races, base exclusion radii and enemy air travel gates are unchanged. Ground-inaccessibility exceptions and counter requirements continue to use physical connectivity, not weakened strength.

Base strength affects ground response from the base only. It does not remove the independent base-proximity exclusion, assumed air counter, or represent a reduction in actual AA/intel coverage. Naval fire support may defend some newly admitted coastal targets; this weighting experiment does not incorporate that support. Combining the existing naval reach with these opportunities remains separate work.

Default assumptions and results

  • Astro and Seton's: land-route strength 100%, base strength 100%.
  • Drunken Beetles: land-route strength 25%, base strength 100%, as a user-requested naval-priority hypothesis. This percentage is a review setting, not a measured property of Beetles.
  • Every map also has all nine route/base combinations at 100%, 50% and 25%, selectable independently in the viewer. Thresholds remain identical across maps.

Beetles' medium preset gains five resource patches: 4 destinations / 4 patches / 15 mexes becomes 6 destinations / 9 patches / 31 mexes. The new patches are on land routes that are geographically close but assumed weak. Strict admits one additional patch at 25% versus 50%. A plateau between weights is expected when resource, base or air gates become limiting.

With the same medium setting, Beetles' expansion destinations change 4 to 6 and gunship destinations 11 to 13. Astro and Seton's full-strength control outputs remain exactly unchanged. Weakening their route assumptions is available for sensitivity analysis but is not applied to their defaults.

Viewer and exports

The viewer adds Land-route strength and Base strength selectors. Switching them changes overlays, candidate checks, economics and the all-map comparison for the selected map. Rows show effective route/base scores alongside physical ETA. Area details report the two strengths and the limiting combined window. The original geometry and route viewer remain available.

data/processed/drop_response_strength_1v1/ contains:

  • comparison.csv: all map/scenario/preset/side/opportunity counts.
  • <map>/response_strength_metrics.csv: all per-area measurements and checks for every scenario.
  • <map>/opportunities.json: nine scenarios, an explicit selected default and baseline-input/code provenance.
  • <map>/opportunity_layers.npz and scenario-prefixed transparent PNG masks.
  • beetles_route_strength_comparison.png: visual sensitivity comparison, medium offense.
  • validation.json: checks on all three maps and all nine scenarios.

Reproduce with PYTHONPATH=src:

.venv-codex/Scripts/python.exe -m supmapai.tools.reweight_drop_responses
# Example: add intermediate or absent-force scenarios
.venv-codex/Scripts/python.exe -m supmapai.tools.reweight_drop_responses --strength-values 1 .75 .5 .25 0 --beetles-route-strength .25

The independent weights accept finite values from zero to one. Initial destination distances are calculated once and cached; scoring variants reuse them. Baseline artifacts stay unchanged. The original extractor still produces full-strength controls; this dedicated tool applies the explicit map hypotheses.

Validation

All 51 targeted tests passed. New cases check weak-route/unchanged-ETA behavior, a strong base veto, an independent route veto, zero-strength semantics, invalid inputs and monotonic strength effects. Actual-data checks confirm exact candidate compatibility at 100%/100%, nested preset sets, monotonic candidate sets as either strength decreases, unchanged physical ETAs, conserved destination membership, PNG/numerical-mask agreement and current source hashes. Heuristic destination counts need not themselves be monotonic when grouping changes; the underlying candidate sets are.
