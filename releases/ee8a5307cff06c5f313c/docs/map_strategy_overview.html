Map strategy analysis: implementation, interpretation and remaining work

22 October labeling proposal: Strategy labels v1 specifies one production configuration, automatic front selection and concrete gameplay ratings so research can close and model training can proceed. It is a proposed release contract; existing report scores are unchanged.

19 October implementation update: the new versioned mass-first pipeline now connects shared mass returns, economic-front placement and contact-response drop/air scenarios for arbitrary local version inputs. Energy is deliberately deferred. The coverage counts below describe the earlier saved reports; a new full-library run has not thereby completed. The pipeline document is authoritative for integration status and retained settings.

Reviewed 19 October 2026 against the saved results, validation reports and source code. This is the current overview; the run log preserves the sequence of experiments. Earlier reports remain historical results unless explicitly regenerated. No new extraction or strategy calculation was performed for this review.

Where the project stands

We can extract useful structural evidence about where players can expand, move armies, contest resources and attack economy from the air. The strongest current interpretation is economic opportunity and response burden under a stated configuration. Actual army placement, intel, AA, combat outcomes and player choices are not known from map files.

There are two different levels of coverage:

  • The collection-wide drop/air analysis attempted all 630 entries and completed
    1. It is a completed earlier model, with known pairing and route-response limitations.
  • The newer own-half frontier model has 29 completed comparison maps. Reclaim, collection tiers and construction-aware economic interpretation have 11 pilot maps. These improvements have not been integrated into and rerun across the collection-wide drop/air report.

Consequently, having a new reclaim/economic panel for a map does not mean that its older drop score already uses those values.

1. Expansion drops, offensive drops and air raids

What each question asks

Analysis Useful interpretation It does not establish
Expansion drop Can transport delivery claim worthwhile economy earlier than an engineer walking, with enough distance/value to justify the transport? That ordinary expansion is valuable only when a drop qualifies, or that the position can be held.
Offensive drop Can a ground payload bypass the likely land response and threaten economic targets before ground defenders can respond effectively? That the enemy lacks AA/intel, the payload survives, or the transport/payload investment pays back in actual combat.
Air raid How much economic geography is exposed to fast air attack under the response assumptions? Total air-superiority importance, including scouting, army support, transport denial and protection of navy.
AA burden How geographically dispersed are targets, and how many sampled coverage positions cover them at a stated range? How many AA units win a fight, their affordability or actual defensive coverage.

The revised drop model requires both reward and a response opportunity. The first speed-only model was rejected because fast flight alone made sites beside Astro's main battle lane look attractive.

Expansion uses hover-engineer walking and unload access. A water mex can be an expansion-drop target without nearby dry shore. Offensive ground payloads still need a dry terrain-feasible landing and land access to their target. Generic air raids may target underwater mexes; this does not imply gunships can shoot them.

The shared medium calibration includes a 96-unit base buffer, normally two mexes per patch, a 22-second-equivalent ground-response window, 12 seconds enemy-air travel and 35 seconds net engineer travel saved after fresh transport production. Inaccessible pockets have explicit resource-count exceptions. The T1 UEF transport reference is 120 mass and 40 seconds production from an existing unassisted factory. Landing patches, clustering and destination grouping remain geometric approximations.

Response strength is an assumption about capacity

Physical walking ETA and defensive strength are separate. For the older response weighting, with a 60-second assessment horizon:

window(t, s) = 60 - s * (60 - min(t, 60))

The limiting base/route window is used. Lower strength admits more opportunity without pretending defenders walk more slowly. These seconds-equivalent are not predicted undefended seconds. The base response multiplier is also different from the route optimizer's base-attraction/snap cost.

The user's resource-distance rule provides a useful land-allocation hypothesis: more valuable economy and shorter enemy approach justify more local land pressure; many separated valuable areas dilute that response. Fractional allocation avoids counting shared economy repeatedly. It still does not reveal actual armies, AA or the mass split between land, air and navy.

Evidence that the direction is useful

  • Astro has no qualifying expansion/offensive/air targets across the calibrated strict/medium/permissive presets. This is a useful compact, well-covered control.
  • The original three-map calibration identifies islands and rear economy on Seton's and Drunken Beetles. Weakening the assumed land response on Beetles adds targets while retaining a strong base response. This supports separating arrival time from available defending strength; it does not measure Beetles' army budget.
  • In the four-map benchmark, deduplicated air-exposed mexes were 82/108 on Adaptive Maridia and 50/90 on Adaptive Point of Reason; Hilly Plateau and Canis River had zero primary-preset targets. The user confirmed the broad contrast, while rejecting the absolute 1–5 interpretation. These are benchmark results, not a fresh test of the later reclaim model.
  • The corrected DualGap slot pair 1/2 reduced targeted offensive/air shares from 62.3%/76.8% to 18.9%/37.7%. Pair selection can matter more than threshold tuning.
  • Pandemonium's earlier nearest-route model produced zero air opportunities. An experimental resource/travel allocation produced 32.3% air exposure. The newer frontier model addresses its multiple economic approaches, but that model has not yet replaced the collection's air-response fields.

Still missing: the construction-aware mex/reclaim ledger has not replaced the older drop payback/denial proxy. Expansion's saved-income calculation is not the new shared-work schedule. Offensive target value is still largely mex-based; valuable reclaim, hydros, factories and upgraded economy do not yet form a unified raid-value model. Naval fire support and AA are not jointly evaluated as defenses.

2. From physical paths to resource-attracted fronts

The distinction we now preserve

  1. Physical navigation: where a movement layer can go and its shortest travel distance. Eight-direction graph edges reject corner cutting. Raw masks remain separate from compressed/culled AI navigation labels.
  2. Deployment path: an economical base-to-front approach attracted toward resources, rather than necessarily the shortest straight push at the enemy.
  3. Response source: where an army is assumed to be stationed. In the latest pilot this is a selected frontier contact or the base, not every point on the deployment trace simultaneously.
  4. Response territory: resources/space most efficiently served from those sources through physical terrain, including fractional ties and Base-preferred resources. It is a service hypothesis, not ownership or guaranteed control.

This prevents a route from wrapping into the enemy half and claiming that friendly units already cover everything near its far end. Behind-base resources are kept as rear economy rather than pulling an attack route past its terminal and back.

The current own-half model cuts on the perpendicular bisector of the selected bases. Only legal terrain edges crossing that frontier create candidate contacts. Every candidate gets the same terminal reward; the deployment trace stops at its first contact. The virtual completion reward never enters the response graph. Enemy resources do not attract the own-half optimizer.

Contacts are selected using resource-weighted physical walking response from a contact or base. A common resource-attracted root tree supplies deployment lines, so shared approaches become explicit M trunks and arbitrary X crossings are avoided. Exact mirrored approaches/territories are displayed. For rotational symmetry, mirrored contacts need not coincide: no fictitious connecting segment is drawn across the frontier.

What route width and count mean

There is no fixed maximum attention/territory width. An open plain can have one very wide approach. Physical bottleneck/clearance width, allocated territory area, shared-trunk length and contact count answer different questions and should stay separate. More contacts needed to saturate economic response is evidence of dispersed service demand; it is not by itself proof of more terrain passages or higher player skill.

Budget K means contacts per side, not K independent full-map attack routes. The latest model assumes a response presence at each contact without charging additional army mass as K rises. Increasing K therefore supplies more response sources; saturation is a coverage-demand curve, not a fixed-army-budget optimum. Deployment length is reported but not charged as army arrival cost in placement.

These values use the same scoped resources within each map, not equal economic quantity across maps. Setons' frontier pool has only three own-side off-base land mexes; its saturation cannot describe the whole naval map.

Why “value if removed” does not sum to 100%

For objective G(S) = weighted walking-access improvement over base-only response, the diagnostic for contact/trace i is:

[G(S) - G(S without i)] / base-only weighted walking cost

Other sources stay fixed. Shared or substitutable coverage loses little when one source disappears. This is marginal unique benefit, not an allocated resource share; the individual losses need not sum to 100%. Splitting one corridor into trace IDs can change these losses without changing total coverage.

The earlier universal 3%/10% weakest-removal stopping rules are not a settled production selector. Neither “low percentage: delete” nor “high percentage: split” alone resolves branch identity, central terrain detours or a broad open front. Current evidence favors reviewing marginal saturation alongside independent resource groups, topology, shared trunks and physical response times. A universal automatic contact-count policy remains undetermined.

3. Resource interpretation: five different quantities

Quantity Question it answers
Physical stock How much known initial mass/energy exists, in which individual objects?
Collection quality How rewarding is an individual operation per engineer-second, relative to alternatives on this map?
Spatial service How easily can a contact/base respond to those resources through terrain?
Collectible economic return What net mass can a finite shared work budget obtain, including mex construction and income after completion?
Military incentive How much force would players invest here, and what could be denied or defended? This is inferred, not directly extracted.

Moving between these levels requires explicit assumptions. A large stock is not instant income; an economic share is not an army share; a high-quality chunk is not automatically contested or reachable. A resource can be safe from a main land approach while difficult to reinforce against air. “Rear,” “remote,” “protected” and “secure against all attacks” are not interchangeable.

Reclaim: identity, handling and valuable cores

The numerical inventory retains separate objects, blueprint identity, initial wreck semantics, positions, known mass/energy and uncertainty. Missing declared numeric reclaim defaults can be zero; unresolved parsing/blueprints remain unknown. Live placed units are not automatically valued as initial wrecks. The Setons initial-wreck total of 11,631.3 mass was checked by the user in game. Tree/rock appearance and gameplay wreck classification are separate attributes.

Under the approved operating hypothesis:

work(m) = m / 25 + 1 and quality(m) = m / work(m)

Same 100 mass Total engineer work Full-clear mass/engineer-s
One 100-mass entity 5 s 20.00
Ten 10-mass entities 14 s 7.14
One hundred 1-mass entities 104 s 0.96

Stacked entities retain separate handling costs. The collection-priority field uses m * m / (m + 25) before spatial aggregation. This is an attention proxy, not a reduction in physical mass or a claim that it is all collected.

Best-first collection curves preserve a valuable core even when a path also contains a large low-value forest. The whole-pool ratio total mass / total work alone can hide that core. Curves show attainable reward versus work and marginal quality as progressively worse objects are taken; travel is still excluded.

Three ordered groups are fitted from mass-weighted log collection quality over known initial positive mass throughout playable bounds. All paths use the same map boundaries. Best on a poor map can have a much lower absolute rate than Best on a rich map, so retain both the tier and absolute throughput. Tiers are not fixed quantiles, do not promise a minimum share, and do not add 3:2:1 economic multipliers. Their association with manual clicks/automatic reclaim is a useful interpretation, not measured player APM or actual patrol behavior.

Adaptive Tabula Rasa illustrates the distinction: its inspected three-group mass shares are approximately 46.44% Best, 14.27% Okay and 39.29% Low priority. This does not make all three stocks equally collectible in five minutes. Protected and rear stock participates in map-level quality calibration without thereby entering the contested land-front resource pool.

Mexes and reclaim: compare finite work and net reward

The latest test provides one existing UEF T1 engineer for 300 seconds, shared across all eligible own-side resources and Base-preferred allocations. Increasing contact count does not manufacture engineers or stock. Reference T1 mex values from the pinned FAF snapshot are 36 mass, 360 energy, 12 seconds construction, 2 mass/s production and 2 energy/s upkeep after completion.

One serial greedy schedule chooses between construction, acquisition/reclaim and financing waits. Existing completed mexes produce while the engineer works. A disclosed 36-mass capital advance permits the first build and is excluded from gain. For each attributed pool:

net mass = mex production after completion - mex construction mass + collected reclaim

Identical jobs receive expected fractional route attribution to avoid task-ID order creating asymmetric importance. They remain serial physical jobs; fractional per-path builds do not mean partial buildings or parallel engineers.

Adaptive Tabula Rasa at four contacts:

  • Builds 8 of 34 eligible mexes and collects 4,750 reclaim mass.
  • Uses 96 seconds construction and 204 seconds reclaim.
  • Produces 3,377.68 mex mass, subtracts 288 construction mass, and yields 7,839.68 combined net mass, versus the old 27,325 instant-mex plus independently budgeted reclaim estimate.
  • F3 contributes 37.4%, F1 26.2%, F4 23.6%, F2 7.0%, Base-preferred 5.8%. Keeping Base explicit explains why route-only shares need not total 100%.
  • Mex construction/upkeep requires 6,257.68 energy, assumed supplied externally.

The useful reclaim share can emerge from this shared schedule rather than a universal “one mex equals X reclaim” rule. At K4, reclaim contributes about 60.6% of Tabula's net mass, 80.7% of this Setons land-front pool and 20.9% on Monument Valley. These are scoped, power-supplied, no-travel economic opportunities.

Economic pull also changes over the match: reclaim depletes while completed mexes keep producing. An attractive early reclaim front need not remain the strongest later front. The current five-minute number is one disclosed time perspective; it does not yet provide a time-varying army-allocation or tech-progression model.

This test is not game-optimal scheduling. It omits travel, worker production, upgrades, combat denial, storage/overflow and pre-existing base income. Construction mass is reserved upfront rather than drained continuously. The fixed budget makes maps comparable under one reference scenario, not equally representative of their actual economies. It currently attributes value to saved layouts; it has not changed their fitted attraction weights or geometry.

Energy: accounted for, not yet fully modeled

Known companion energy from a scheduled mass-bearing object is credited from the same operation, without a second handling charge. Construction/upkeep costs, final energy demand, peak cumulative deficit and unknown companion values are exported. Power is assumed supplied; the schedule cannot yet stall for energy.

Energy-only props are retained in the inventory but not selected by the mass-focused schedule. There are 8,419 known positive-energy/zero-mass entities across the 11 playable-area inventories, including 7,241 on Pandemonium. Ignoring them would lose a real resource layer.

Before energy efficiency tiers, audit matching pinned FAF processing rules, engineer build rate and prop reclaim-time multipliers. The inspected current FAF source suggests a joint-resource operation; this has not yet been validated against the pinned timing source used for this dataset. Do not assume energy/25, charge acquisition twice, add independent full mass/energy work budgets, or invent a universal mass:energy exchange rate. Hydro investment, energy income, generators, shortages and overflow also need an explicit economic context.

4. What is clear, and what still needs evidence

Conclusions we can use now

  • Configuration, resource activation and correct opponents are first-order inputs. Team-game intuition cannot directly validate a two-active-slot result.
  • Terrain access, shortest travel, resource position/quantity, separate entity handling and bookkeeping conservation are useful deterministic features.
  • Deployment and response are different. Treating a line as an army everywhere along it systematically overprotects economic space.
  • Resource attraction, shared trunks, exact qualified symmetry, endpoint capture and own-half response address observed geometry failures better than purely shortest-path routing.
  • Reclaim needs entity-aware collection and a valuable-core view. Raw total mass, density or full-clear average alone is insufficient.
  • Mex flow and finite reclaim can be compared over a disclosed work/time scenario, with construction and energy accounting. There is no context-free exchange rate.
  • Air/drop exposure, naval range potential and land-front structure should remain separate features before any attempted overall gameplay interpretation.

5. Community evidence and model-training use

The historical survey snapshot contains 267 latest participant/map reviews from 23 contributors across 186 maps. Exact-version overlap with the earlier cohort is 44 maps/97 reviews, only 14 marked 1v1. Most matched maps have one reviewer; only Setons and DualGap have at least three numeric answers to the compared questions. Slots are generally unspecified, and team setups dominate repeated examples.

The earlier small 1v1 subset had Spearman agreement of 0.83 for air (10 maps), 0.60 for drops (nine) and -0.34 for width-based openness (eight connected-land maps). Experienced-only drop agreement was 0.00 across seven maps. These are historical diagnostic observations, not current-model accuracy or a stable validation claim. User feedback used to develop the algorithm is calibration evidence; the same maps are no longer blind tests.

Setons illustrates land choke versus wide naval fronts and 1v1 versus 4v4. DualGap illustrates pairing and 1v1 versus 6v6 resource/defense dilution. Hardshield's single high air rating is not consensus, and “air importance” is broader than economy raiding. Survey expansion importance is not expansion-drop importance. Subjective gameplay quality is not inferred from these structural outputs. Historical absolute 1–5 formulas and cohort-percentile 1–5 scores are different scales; neither is yet calibrated to community ratings.

For training and data checks, preserve three layers of evidence:

  1. Extracted facts and provenance: raw values/masks/entities, IDs, version, source hashes, configuration, unknowns and exclusions.
  2. Modeled features: travel, contact demand, economic scenarios, exposure, assumptions, algorithm version and applicable movement/resource scope.
  3. Human judgements: original distributions and review configuration, kept separate from model predictions and suggestion-exposed feedback.

Missing/unresolved is not zero, a percentile is not probability, and an excluded map is not low quality. Keep related versions/map families in the same evaluation split; calibrating and testing on nearly identical revisions would overstate generalization. Runtime-heavy adaptive options, team setups and richer biome/ texture semantics remain later work. Current scope does not establish tech-tier combat impact, full economy safety or automatic gameplay quality.

6. Practical next milestone

A reasonable next milestone is one coherent 1v1 strategy record per map:

  • reviewed pair/default configuration and versioned physical movement layers;
  • land contact saturation, deployment/shared sections and scoped resources;
  • fixed-policy reclaim inventory/quality and construction-aware mass/energy data;
  • expansion, offensive and generic-air targets using the same resource and response generation, with raw timings and rejected-candidate reasons;
  • separate naval/hover coverage and unresolved features, without a forced overall quality or universal 1–5 score.

First demonstrate this on the approved controls and several held-out maps, then refresh the frozen collection and annotation comparison. Acceptance should include numerical/PNG agreement, legal movement, symmetry, conserved ownership/work, dependency hashes and in-game spot checks. A new full-library run before these pieces are connected would mostly create another incompatible report generation.
