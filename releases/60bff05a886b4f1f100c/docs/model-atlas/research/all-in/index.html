All source layers at once: a concluded negative result

MapGen research report · 25 October 2026 · Research closed by user decision

The straightforward “all in” experiment was implemented and run, but did not produce usable full-map generation. A single 73.3-million-parameter diffusion model predicted terrain, water, both starts, resources, natural reclaim, wrecks, civilians and RGB appearance together, conditioned on twelve gameplay ratings. It received no clean spatial conditions or derived analytical layers. About 9.10 hours of measured training-session time on one local RTX 5080, including controlled follow-up diagnostics, did not solve the task.

This is a negative result for the tested representation, objective, optimization and compute budget. It is not proof that joint diffusion is impossible. The same network could memorize one map's terrain and point locations in twelve minutes, while still producing incorrect absolute quantities. The evidence points to optimization instability and a mismatch between the sparse-density objective and useful generation. It does not establish a need for a larger model or justify weeks of unchanged training.

The experimental checkpoints are preserved as historical evidence. None is selected for the working staged generator. No map from this experiment was exported or playtested. No further training is scheduled as part of this research.

1. Question and practical success criteria

The question was deliberately simple: if we give one model all basic source layers and map ratings together, and use the available GPU intensively, will it discover the necessary relationships without the staged pipeline's extra layers and constraints?

The requested result was a complete set of generated heatmaps, plus rating controls. Useful progress therefore needed to appear in fresh generation from noise: coherent terrain and water, two localized starts, plausible marker and reclaim layouts, reasonable quantities, and recognizable surface appearance. A falling denoising loss alone would not satisfy that goal. Exact thresholds for every quality dimension were not preregistered; the clear failures below are observable without choosing a marginal pass/fail threshold after the fact.

“All in” describes the simultaneous source-layer experiment. It does not mean an exhaustive architecture search or all possible hardware. We used the reserved local GPU, profiled capacity, and chose a network/batch configuration that kept it busy. We did not train a family of larger architectures, sweep dataset sizes, or search many objectives.

2. Data: what was actually available and included

The corrected source cohort contained 1,117 curated map families, with 1,020 training / 97 validation examples. The scenario was a selected 10 km, 1v1 configuration with two active starts, one representative per family. Exact identities and family splits were matched across terrain, resources, typed reclaim and texture releases. The model did not consume arbitrary mixtures of tensor sizes.

After joining and auditing these sources, the sealed joint release contained 851 maps: 782 train / 69 validation, with no family overlap. There was no independent test set used. Exclusion reasons overlapped: 188 incomplete/unsupported RGB, 100 unknown wreck mass, five incomplete rating vectors, two unknown natural-energy values and two unknown natural-mass values. These removed 266 unique examples. A preliminary 853-map candidate lost two more examples when natural-value completeness was checked.

The final joint release had positive hydro targets on 824 maps, natural energy on 752, natural mass on 820, wreck mass on 180 and civilians on 140. Both starts and mexes were present on all 851. This imbalance matters: rare and empty output roles were part of the actual learning problem.

The dedicated civilian extraction covered 24,968 in-canvas source entities on 217 maps, including 201 train / 16 validation maps in the original cohort. Five outside/invalid placements were excluded. Explicitly inactive source records were omitted. A zero civilian layer means no identified source placement; it does not resolve every lobby option, adaptive script or runtime activation rule. Unit/building identity, footprint and asset choice were not prediction targets.

RGB was a simplification requested for this experiment. It was the saved unlit material appearance, excluding decals, lighting, props, normal-map shading and rendered water. It was not a screenshot of the finished map, and its successful extraction does not mean that a learned RGB image could directly be exported as the original materials.

3. Exact representation and model interface

Every example supplied a 13 × 256 × 256 target in this fixed order:

Channels Output meaning Source-to-grid construction
1: height Terrain height relative to source reference Four-corner cell mean of native vertices, then area pooling
2: water_depth Physical water depth Pooling of saved native physical depth
3–4: start_a, start_b One start per channel Unit-integral Gaussian per exact start coordinate, sigma 1.5 cells
5–6: mex, hydro Resource-marker count density Unit-integral Gaussians, sigma 1.5 cells
7–8: natural_energy, natural_mass Absolute natural reclaim values per cell Source tree/rock/boulder/reclaim props, value-weighted Gaussians, sigma 1 cell
9: wreck_mass Absolute initial wreck mass per cell Separate source wreck entities, sigma 1 cell
10: civilians Source civilian entity count density Living civilian unit/building records, sigma 1.5 cells
11–13: red, green, blue Unlit surface RGB Saved supported material extraction

Boundary handling preserved raster totals. Targets retained absolute amounts: they were not normalized to one shared budget per map. Both physical and encoded float32 arrays were saved. The loader cached encoded targets in float16 on the CPU and converted batches to float32; model compute used bfloat16. Release encode/decode checks had maximum relative error 7.07 × 10⁻⁷, before loader-cache quantization.

Height used a reversible asinh transform, asinh(height / 4) / scale. Nonnegative fields used 2 * log1p(quantity) / scale - 1. RGB used 2 * rgb - 1. Per-channel scales were fitted from positive training-map samples only, at percentile 99.5 with up to 512 sampled values per map/channel. Source values were not clipped to that percentile. Saved normalization records the actual constants and sampler support.

At generation time the network received only Gaussian noise of shape 13 × 256 × 256, a diffusion timestep, and twelve rating scalars. Each rating was normalized as (score - 3) / 2:

expansion_drop_rating, offensive_drop_rating, air_raid_rating, drop_impact_rating, air_mobility_relevance, land_attack_relevance, naval_attack_relevance, mex_supply_rating, reclaim_abundance_rating, early_economy_rating, front_openness_rating, army_control_demand_rating.

These were automated, policy-specific gameplay scores, not community votes or general quality labels. The model did not receive the source map, terrain anchors, SDF, pathing, fronts, access/economy fields, opportunity grids, blueprint IDs, texture masks or palettes. There were no spatial validity/eligibility inputs or loss-exclusion masks. Incomplete examples were excluded before the release was sealed. Target-dependent foreground loss weights are described separately below; “no additional masks” does not mean “no weighting in the loss.”

4. Architecture, optimization and sampling

Item Actual configuration
Network Residual five-scale conditional U-Net, 73,323,149 parameters
Width Base 128, multipliers 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 4, embedding width 384
Spatial processing Two residual blocks per level; attention at 32 × 32 and 16 × 16
Input / output 13 noisy channels → 13 predicted velocity channels; zero clean spatial-condition channels
Scalar conditioning Twelve-score MLP; learned null embedding; 10% whole-profile dropout for classifier-free guidance
Diffusion Cosine schedule, 1,000 timesteps, velocity prediction
Optimizer AdamW, weight decay 0.01 for full-cohort training; gradient norm clipped to 1
Main learning rate 0.0002, 500-update warmup, then constant; no decay schedule tested
Precision bfloat16 forward compute, float32 parameters and optimizer state
Batch Microbatch 6 × four accumulated microsteps = effective batch 24
Augmentation Aligned rotations/reflections across all layers, plus start-channel swaps
Other regularization Feature dropout 0.1 and EMA weights; EMA decay increases up to 0.999
Default fresh sampling DDIM, 48 steps, guidance 1.5, matched seeds including 303 and 404

The 16-map implementation check used a higher LR of 0.0003, disabled augmentation/dropout and zero weight decay. Later one-map diagnostic arms used effective batch six and disabled augmentation, feature dropout and rating dropout. These settings should not be mistaken for the full-cohort configuration.

The main objective averaged the thirteen channel losses equally. Within the eight sparse entity/value channels, pixel weights were 1 + 12 * clamp((encoded_target + 1) / 2, 0, 1), normalized by each channel's mean weight. Height, depth and RGB had uniform weights. There were no auxiliary losses enforcing start count, global quantities, shoreline consistency, passability, symmetry or gameplay validity.

Default sampling bounded intermediate clean estimates using training-range support, with a 20% height margin and 20% upper margin for quantity channels. Raw unbounded final estimates and bounded versions were saved separately. Thus this was not a completely unconstrained numerical sampler, even though it had no analytical spatial conditions. Reported physical totals below come from raw final estimates. No gameplay repair, count caps, budget normalization, symmetry projection, placement decoder or asset composer was applied.

Checkpoints preserved model, EMA, Adam state, random state, dataset/configuration identities and frozen code snapshots. Continuations were actual resumes rather than fresh models relabeled as longer training. Main configuration, training receipt, training implementation.

6. Main experiment: loss improved, generation failed

Implementation check and pilot

The four-minute, 16-map implementation check reduced fixed-noise loss from 1.02605 to 0.06942, with improvement in every channel. Because evaluation reused training maps, this established only that the implementation could optimize the joint objective.

The separately initialized full-cohort pilot reached update 2,074 in about 42 minutes. Held-out t=500 denoising loss fell from 1.01603 to 0.0274457, a 97.3% reduction. Fresh outputs nevertheless had blurred terrain/appearance and excessive count/value densities. Early scalar response existed: a matched-seed mex-supply request of 1 versus 5 changed mex density integral from 442.7 to 703.5. That is evidence of sensitivity, not calibrated control or a validated relationship for all twelve knobs.

Long continuation and instability

The continuation preserved the pilot's model/EMA/optimizer/RNG state; its initial validation exactly reproduced 0.0274457. It ended at update 22,947, with final/best t=500 loss 0.0228252. Looking only at those endpoints would conceal what happened:

This was not a clean, smoothly flattening learning curve. Much of the added compute was spent losing and recovering an earlier solution. Longer training did eventually lower the recorded loss, but the final fresh-map evidence did not establish progress toward a useful generator.

Terrain and RGB retained cloudy, weakly organized structure. Reclaim often filled broad areas. That is evidence of background bias, not a trustworthy claim that the generated map is physically deep water everywhere. Water metrics depended on thresholds, and no engine/playability conclusion is drawn from them.

7. Controlled diagnosis after stopping the long run

All four training arms completed. The same 73.3M network, frozen dataset/encoding and thirteen outputs were retained; no analytical spatial inputs or production repair stages were added. The three one-map arms started from matching fresh initialization/seed and used map 4375_6603, which includes every output role. The full-cohort arm resumed the preserved pilot update 2,074, rather than the late recovered model.

This result rules out the strongest claim that the network cannot learn spatial structure at all. It does not show that it can learn the full distribution from the available data, or that its generated quantities are suitable for export.

Did lower LR help stability?

The full-cohort controlled restart changed the learning rate from 0.0002 to 0.00005, retaining the original loss, augmentation, effective batch, model/EMA/Adam state and random-state lineage. Over one hour it reached update 4,971, crossing the original failure point at 4,412 without the loss spike. Its held-out loss decreased smoothly from 0.0274457 to 0.0233471. The final value was only 2.29% above the old long run's 0.0228252 after roughly 6.7 additional hours.

This is evidence that optimization stability was a major avoidable cost in the original run. It is one controlled restart window, not proof of indefinite stability or a complete identification of the collapse's cause. LR decay, optimizer alternatives, larger backbones and repeated restarts were not tested.

Fresh full-cohort maps were still blurred and overpopulated. At seed 303, Start A/B integrals were 152.38 / 120.57, mex 513.97 and civilians 1,317.36. Better stability did not solve the representation/objective problem. Saved lower-LR output.

Did emphasizing foreground solve sparse outputs?

The objective audit found that nonzero Start A target activity covered only 0.46% of its actual loader-weighted pixel mass, despite the foreground boost. Most weighted pixels still described background. A simple empty nonnegative-layer baseline had very small unweighted reconstruction errors on starts/civilians. That makes aggregate losses poor proxies for localization and meaningful quantities. The baseline's clean-MSE metric is not interchangeable with the network's weighted velocity objective.

The balanced objective assigned half the nonempty entity-channel loss to ordinary pixel error and half to source-activity-normalized error; empty channels used ordinary MSE. It changed loss weights, not inputs or target layers. Compared with the same lower LR and time budget, foreground MSE improved 52.6%, from 0.01849 to 0.00877. However, Start A integral worsened from 170.10 to 191.82. Local foreground fit and global quantity calibration are separate problems. This variant was not a successful fix and was not promoted to a full training release.

Numerical bounding contributed to bias, but the tested sampler changes did not recover usable maps. Removing bounds produced negative quantity predictions on about 23% of evaluated quantity pixels, so a near-zero signed total could not count as success. Full sampling comparison, frozen-checkpoint results.

A source-aware oracle reconstructed the real target with float32 sampler algebra; focused tests also checked exact equivalence to the original sampler. Artificial bfloat16 velocity rounding alone changed start integrals by about 0.2%. Artificial input/output rounding caused roughly 15–41% errors in that simplified oracle, depending on bounds, rather than the observed hundredfold excess. This does not exclude every learned-network precision interaction, but sampler algebra or rounding alone is not an adequate account of the observed failure.

Additional validation at timesteps 100, 500, 900 and 990 reproduced the saved t=500 result and showed greater reconstruction error toward nearly pure noise. Those tests strengthen the distinction between denoising an existing source and synthesizing a coherent complete map from noise. They do not establish a generative-quality score.

8. Conclusions and limits

Claim Conclusion supported by this experiment
“Throw all source layers and ratings into one model and sufficient local compute solves it.” Not supported. This implementation consumed substantial compute and failed to generate useful complete maps.
“The network cannot learn a map at all.” Contradicted by memorization. A fixed map's terrain and peak positions were learned quickly.
“The long curve simply reached a normal plateau.” Incorrect description. It underwent a large deterioration and spent hours recovering.
“More time on the unchanged setup is justified.” Not by the recorded evidence. Lower-LR diagnostics were more efficient, while fresh output and totals remained poor.
“More parameters are required.” Not established. No model-size comparison was performed.
“Foreground weighting fixes it.” Not established. Local fit improved, global totals worsened.
“The rating knobs work.” Only limited sensitivity observed. One pilot mex sweep responded; broad calibration, independence and useful controllability were not demonstrated.
“Joint diffusion cannot work.” Not established. The negative result is bounded to this data, representation, objective, optimization and budget.

The earlier staged models provided more encouraging visual structure within short runs, as observed during the project. This experiment supports retaining that working staged path. It is not a controlled proof that staging is universally superior: staged models receive different spatial information, solve narrower tasks, use different representations and can depend on deterministic composition. We did not run an equal-budget matched comparison in this study.

Important limits are the small curated distribution, a reduced 851-map complete-source cohort, rare civilian/wreck positives, automated rating semantics, no independent final test set, few generated seeds and no systematic diversity/nearest-neighbor study. Validation was repeatedly consulted for research decisions. There was no engine validation, export, balance test, broad rating calibration, architecture scaling law or estimate of weeks-to-success.

If this direction is reopened, the evidence suggests first testing quantity/background representations and global-total objectives under stable optimization, with progressive small-dataset fresh generation. Those are future hypotheses, not implemented fixes or scheduled training. Research is closed now; no claim is made that these changes would necessarily succeed.

Recorded outcome: the “all in” approach was tried seriously on the available GPU and did not solve MapGen's complete generation task. The useful result is a preserved failure case with evidence separating learnability, instability, sparse-layer calibration and fresh generation.
