Future work

Current strategy status, interpretation and prioritized integration work are in the map-strategy overview, reviewed 19 October 2026. Completed experiments remain in their reports; a future-work item is not a claim that those reports already use the newest model.

Adaptive map settings beyond MVP

Preserve the extracted option catalog and audited rules as research work. Defer questionnaire settings controls, alternate-setting render libraries, and diffusion-model conditioning on adaptive options. Adaptive Diversity is the reference example: configurable mexes/hydros, wrecks, civilian groups, natural reclaim multipliers, tree regrowth and Crazyrush.

For the general annotation preview library, render lobby defaults with all recorded player slots assumed open. The separate 1v1 strategy analysis resolves defaults for its two selected active slots; do not interchange those resource sets. Show numbered spawns and supported script-declared resource links. Record unsupported activation rules and uncertain reclaim explicitly. Do not generate a Cartesian product of adaptive configurations.

Future training work should preserve independent entities, resource ownership, option dependencies and both appearance and resource value. Validate additional script families against in-game behavior before treating them as training labels.

Reclaim collection calibration

The operating policy is now fixed at 1s per entity and three map-relative groups. Preserve historical delay profiles as research; do not resume sweeps or automatic group-count selection for routine computation. The old 0.5s comparison reference is not the operating default or a measured value. Compare equal-total-mass groups within one engineer's reach to minimize movement, and measure sparse-object travel separately. Engineer routing, coordination and depletion schedules remain outside the current static frontier model. See collection effort methodology.

Resource economy and strategy integration

Historical pilot: an 11-map fixed-policy reclaim inventory/tier export and a shared 300s engineer schedule including mex construction, income after completion, construction mass and a separate energy ledger. See the economic pilot. This attributes value to saved front layouts; it has not replaced route-fit weights or collection-wide drop scoring.

The versioned mass-first pipeline now connects that schedule to front placement and contact-based drop/air response. Energy is deliberately deferred at the user's request. Collection-wide regeneration and validation remain separate from implementing the pipeline.

Next work, in order:

  1. Run the desired library selection through the new pipeline and inspect exclusions after the recorded benchmark/cache checks. Preserve the three balance settings, response strengths, explicit contact budgets and1s/three-tier operating policy.
  2. When energy work resumes, audit pinned joint mass/energy reclaim timing and prop multipliers. Include energy-only targets and hydros in an explicit shared-work economic context; avoid double handling charges or a universal mass:energy exchange rate.
  3. Validate finite-window work/travel, starting economy and response-budget assumptions. Determine a contact saturation policy without treating every contact as an independent full-map route or fitting counts to known maps.
  4. Extend latest fronts to hover/amphibious and relate naval support to economic exposure. Reduced UEF naval profiles are coded; archived outputs are T1-only.
  5. Refresh exact-version community comparisons with blind, exact-slot 1v1 reviews; keep team configurations and suggested responses identifiable.
