Direct terrain controls v1

Implemented 23 October 2026. These are resource-independent continuous measurements, separate from frozen strategy-label pipeline 1.2.1. Historical labels and checkpoints remain unchanged.

Review the 22-case comparison: six synthetic cases, eight familiar maps, four existing generated samples and four actual encoded model targets. CSV, JSONL targets, width profiles, source/code hashes and PNGs accompany it. The final comparison took 41.4 seconds on CPU; no training, new generation or collection-wide relabeling was started.

Measurements

Terrain relief Playable height p95 minus p05 Robust range including underwater terrain, without isolated extreme heights dominating

No aggregate “openness” score or certified attack-lane count is produced.

Inputs and observability

Inputs are height, water state/coverage, playable bounds, physical cell size and two supplied base coordinates. There are no mexes, reclaim, faction stats, economic weights or route witnesses in the calculation. The selected pair is still required: this is a two-base structural measurement, not a spawn-independent or team-game label.

The height/water adapter uses strict four-corner slope checks below 0.75 per map unit and water exclusion. Source terrain-type codes, props, units and scripted obstacles are deliberately omitted because those are not channels of the current terrain-only model. This is not a substitute for full native game-pathability validation.

Blocked/disconnected bases produce known false connectivity and null width/bottleneck targets. Outside-playable or coincident grid bases are unresolved. Disconnection is not a zero-width openness score.

Resource-free cross sections

Compute shortest-distance fields from each base on their common connected land component, using eight-neighbor movement without corner cutting. Define:

phase(x) = (distance_from_A(x) − distance_from_B(x)) / 2

This progress coordinate advances in physical distance along a shortest A–B path. Its sections bend around terrain instead of using a single representative route or resource-attracted line. A constant-phase offshoot does not add crossing edges merely because it is reachable.

For each sampled level, find passable grid faces crossing that level. Orientation correction reduces diagonal staircase bias. Connected face groups provide individual geometric spans; retain their total length, largest span and complete width list. Typical width is the median in the central 50% of shortest-distance progress, restricted to interior samples.

The result is aggregate traversable cross-section length. Folded/curved sections can be longer than their straight-line projection. Effective span count, (sum widths)^2 / sum(widths^2), is a diagnostic of partitioning, not a military lane count. Spans can split or merge between sections. A small obstacle can split a section without creating another worthwhile attack lane. This diagnostic count is not a conditioning target.

Bottlenecks need both absolute width and persistence

Ignore the first/last 32 shortest-distance units near the bases and sample approximately every map unit. Record minimum widths and the smallest mean over eight units, separately for total width and the largest span.

relative_contraction = clip(1 − sustained_minimum_total_width / typical_total_width, 0, 1)

Also retain largest-span contraction, the longest run below half the typical width, and the fraction of the interior occupied by such runs lasting at least eight units. Persistent fragmentation similarly requires effective spans of at least 1.5 for at least eight units.

A uniformly narrow corridor can have low contraction: it has no additional local pinch, but its absolute widths are still small. Several narrow spans can have moderate total width while each span remains restrictive. Read these together rather than collapsing them into one scalar.

Distances here are geodesic progress, not wall thickness. Even a thin wall with a gap can create a funnel. Buffers, eight-unit support and half-typical thresholds are explicit measurement conventions, not proven unit-formation thresholds.

Existing generated samples use their saved requested bases, not guessed coordinates.

The full suite passed 381 tests. The comparison's validation.json also checks all 22 preview files, target masks, per-section span conservation and pinned implementation hashes, and verifies that all frozen strategy-release artifacts remain unchanged.

Native maps versus what the model sees

Native and model-representation labels are not interchangeable. Downsampling, clipping, interpolation and water reconciliation can change narrow obstacles or connectivity.

The joint_target adapter decodes the actual three target channels and applies the existing deterministic height/water reconciliation plus bilinear native reconstruction. Disabled water remains dry. No old resource-conditioning profile, hidden source detail, learned refinement, smoothing or forced symmetry enters the adapter.

Twin Rivers' largest span changes from 240.5 on the native source to 350.5 on its decoded training representation. Pandemonium's total/largest widths change from 319.0/119.1 to 368.7/155.7. This is representation loss, not a reason to supervise a terrain-only model with native details it cannot see.

For new training, derive controls from the exact terrain representation and decoder the model learns. Keep native measurements as a separate source/game-geometry evaluation. For generated outputs, evaluate raw reconstruction and later refinement/smoothing separately: postprocessing can change the controls.

Target schema and integration boundary

targets.jsonl exports terrain_condition_targets_v1: raw continuous values and loss masks for:

water_fraction
terrain_relief
base_connectivity
front_total_width
front_largest_span_width
front_fragmentation_fraction
bottleneck_total_width
bottleneck_largest_span_width
bottleneck_relative_contraction
bottleneck_restricted_length

There is no economic front_width, resource/army-control rating, witness count or claimed attack-lane count. Complete profiles remain available for inspection.

Native/model rows retain family and split identity where available. Synthetic/generated cases are diagnostic, not training examples. No normalizer or presets were fitted on this mixed review set. Future scaling must use train only, retaining families and masks. Positive widths/lengths/relief can use log1p; fractions/connectivity should be handled separately.

Existing checkpoints and joint_terrain_v1 data retain their original 13-dimensional conditioning. They do not understand these new controls automatically. A new conditioning manifest/normalizer and versioned trainer/sampler wiring are required before retraining. That rebuild and collection-wide measurements have deliberately not been launched during this bounded review. Historical economic labels remain useful for resource-aware models.

Code and bounded reproduction

Core: src/supmapai/terrain_controls/measure.py, conditioning.py, joint_target.py. CLI: supmapai.tools.measure_terrain_controls. Tests: tests/test_terrain_controls.py.

$env:PYTHONPATH = 'src'
$env:MPLCONFIGDIR = (Resolve-Path .local-tools/matplotlib).Path
.venv-codex/Scripts/python.exe -m supmapai.tools.measure_terrain_controls `
  --out data/processed/terrain_controls_new_review `
  --generated data/models/terrain_steering_10km_v1 `
  --joint-source data/tensors/terrain_joint_10km_1v1

Use a fresh output directory. Defaults select eight familiar maps; repeated --key selects another bounded set. Settings, source signatures and code hashes are recorded. Hard cell/section bounds raise explicit errors rather than silently coarsening resolution.

Remaining limits: grid/gradient approximation, finite spacing and endpoint buffer, geometric fragmentation rather than certified lanes, no prop/unit/script collision model, and dependence on pair and represented resolution. This pilot checks correctness and interpretation; it does not establish exhaustive topology or universal gameplay validity.
