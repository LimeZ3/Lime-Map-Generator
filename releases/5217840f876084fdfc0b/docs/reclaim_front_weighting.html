Separate mex and reclaim fronts

This experiment adds initial mass reclaim as a separate spatial objective to the accepted own-half frontier-contact model. It preserves the earlier mex-only collection and does not set a new route-count cutoff.

Economic scale

The map supplies a stock of reclaim and a flow of mex income. Comparing a stock with a count of mexes requires an economic time window; geometry alone cannot identify the unique correct window or actual match economy.

  • R: known initial mass stock within the playable rectangle.
  • I: summed T1 income proxy of all configured active mex markers, including starting economy and underwater mexes. This remains an explicit proxy, not actual occupied, upgraded or growing economy.
  • A: playable area in world units squared.
  • T = sqrt(A) / 3.4: reference map-length travel window, using the existing model's land response speed. It is not an engineer trip or collection-time simulation.
  • R / I: how many seconds of that mex income the known stock represents.
  • R / (I T): dimensionless stock/flow balance over this window.
  • w = R / (R + I T): bounded reclaim weight. Above one half means the known stock exceeds the reference-window production; it does not establish greater gameplay importance in every stage of the match.

The land-front objective uses eligible stock on both actual sides, R_front, instead of the whole-map R. It excludes protected starting economy, rear expansions, neutral frontier cells and targets disconnected within their half. Water/blocked origins are accessible only if a legal land tile lies within the recorded five-unit reclaim reach. Every known mass unit appears exactly once in the scope ledger. The global whole-map weight and land-front weight are both exported so that a water-rich map is not described as reclaim-poor overall.

The viewer compares 0.5T, T and 2T, as well as pure mex and pure reclaim controls. Windows are explicit sensitivity settings, not fitted thresholds. No weights were adjusted to force an expected number of corridors.

Spatial density

Raw mass is preserved per world-unit grid cell; stacked objects add their full known values. There is no clipping of rich piles or cap on target count. Mass distribution directly affects the reclaim-access objective, so an equal stock concentrated in one place and spread across several flanks can require different contact counts.

Density diagnostics use a scale equal to half mean mex spacing, sigma = sqrt(A/N_mex)/2. The local stock/flow field is:

local_w = smoothed_mass / (smoothed_mass + smoothed_mex_income * T)

Orange indicates local stock exceeds local production over the same window; blue indicates the reverse. Empty influence regions are marked separately. The export also records mean mass and mex density, effective occupied density area, concentration relative to uniform stock, and the fraction of mass in reclaim-dominant regions. These are descriptive fields, not an additional multiplier rewarding the same mass twice.

Contact objective

Each layer keeps an independent base-response baseline. Let G_mex and G_reclaim be the respective income- or mass-weighted fractional response-cost reductions. The combined objective is:

G = (1-w) * G_mex + w * G_reclaim

Both component scores, response times and per-contact allocations remain visible. Mex marker counts are distinct from their income-weighted values. Reclaim allocation is mass, not prop count. Base-preferred value remains explicit and allocations conserve each layer's total.

The budget table evaluates every layout at the same default map-context weight for fair comparisons. The CSV also retains the score under each profile's own optimization weight. Scores computed at different weights must not be ranked as though they shared one objective.

The physical movement graph, first-frontier-contact termination, exact primary symmetry and shared deployment trunks remain unchanged. Reclaim attraction uses a spatial Gaussian with the existing 64-unit attraction scale; it can prefer nearby stock across a barrier but cannot open an edge or change physically scored walking distance. Candidate-to-target graph distances are streamed and cached by physical graph and target coordinates, without retaining a full grid for every prop.

Pilot and limits

Seven contrasting maps: original Loki v3, Adaptive Pyramid, Pandemonium Pass, Morning Prayer, DualGap Adaptive, Wonder Open and Setons. Each has five weight profiles and budgets one through eight. See the local report.

Numerical extraction reuses the pinned blueprint index and FAF wreck rules, with the same two active slots and selected defaults. Initial props and supported wreck groups are valued; live units are not turned into wrecks. Future battle wrecks, runtime-created reclaim, regrowth, collection throughput, technology, economy growth and army-strength dilution are not simulated. Unresolved values remain unknown. Original Loki's scripted permanent wall collision is still absent from the terrain navigation estimate.

This is an inspectable weighting hypothesis. Validation of geometry and arithmetic does not establish that these economic windows predict player decisions; compare the sensitivity profiles and community feedback before turning the blend into training labels.
