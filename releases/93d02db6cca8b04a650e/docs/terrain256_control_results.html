Full256 Direct-control Test

Run: data/models/terrain_full256_control_test_v1. Checkpoint: validation-selected terrain_full256_controls_v3/best.pt, EMA. 32 samples: four seeds (1001-1004), eight profiles, all 10 km 1v1. Generation took 34.4 seconds; CPU measurement/reporting took 110.4 seconds. No training, map installation or game launch was performed.

Method

Each seed starts from the same PCG noise across every profile. Base positions are fixed at (96,256) and (416,256). Sampling uses 100 DDIM steps and guidance 1.5. Water request (20%) and relief request (training median) are held fixed. The current learned-water-budget symmetry decoder and light smoothing are used for final terrain; no resource pads, passage carving or base relocation is applied.

Profile presets use the frozen training-only normalization. Widths are adjusted together to avoid impossible tuples: sustained total bottleneck equals typical total front times (1 - contraction), with a compatible largest-span width. Weak versus strong bottleneck profiles also request shorter versus longer restriction duration. These are linked profile bundles, not one-coordinate causal ablations. Disconnected requests set connectivity to zero and mask unavailable width fields, matching the training representation rather than inventing zero-width fronts.

Raw model outputs are reconstructed with measure_joint_target, exactly as in dataset labeling. Final symmetric/smoothed native heights are measured with the same measure_vertices teacher. Physical values, masks, statuses and full cross-section evidence are retained for both stages. No route-witness counts or economic/front-resource proxies are used.

Results

Four seeds establish an exploratory response, not statistical reliability.

The front-width request is 245.4 versus 481.3 world units, so the final mean change of 9.8 units is far too small to claim calibrated front control. Largest uninterrupted span does move more clearly (213.5 -> 308.6); the network often changes fragmentation instead of creating a substantially different total front.

The dedicated fragmentation profiles produce final largest spans of 343.9 versus 221.2 world units while total width stays around 390. This is consistent with partitioning the front, but is not certified control over an attack-lane count. Changes to local hills/roughness can influence these metrics too.

Strong bottleneck requests ask for contraction 0.681 versus 0.327 and restricted length 46 versus zero. Output contraction and persistence do not track that requested separation. A few tiny positive differences are not success.

All connected baseline requests remain connected. All disconnected requests also remain connected, even before postprocessing. This failure therefore cannot be attributed solely to the symmetry decoder. Nearest training-profile distances are retained as support diagnostics, not probabilities or a substitute for checking spatial support and training-label quality.

Recommended Follow-up

Before another long training run, isolate which final processing operations attenuate the raw front response: symmetry projection, water reconciliation and light smoothing. Compare geometry-preserving alternatives on these same saved samples without regenerating noise or choosing only flattering examples.

Separately, bottleneck and connectivity conditioning need improvement in the learner/data balance or structural objective; their raw-model failures are not fixed by changing the final decoder alone. More compute is not yet an established solution. No retraining was started automatically.
