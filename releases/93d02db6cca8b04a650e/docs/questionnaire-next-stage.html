Collection provenance and priorities

The original 6,047-map questionnaire catalog came from the owner's manually selected local library. Selection used previews and a judgment of dataset usefulness, not a verified positive-quality label. Preserve that provenance. The October 8 vault snapshot contains 14,811 parent map records and 32,213 map versions; these are different units, so the download backlog is not simply 14,811 minus 6,047. The questionnaire also contains a small number of vault-only popular representatives, whose metadata must not be mistaken for an audited local map. Record local presence, download status, audit status, and source separately.

Compute the exact missing set by parent ID and exact version ID/folder, joining all local inventory records first. Select one version per parent for initial coverage and retain a manifest of historical versions for subsequent work. Preserve readable hidden/unranked records as provenance, while keeping public questionnaire visibility a deliberate choice. Failed downloads remain missing, not zero-size maps. Do not label maps negative merely because they were omitted from the manual selection, unranked, unpopular, hidden or failed extraction.

Keep the curated library and ranked popular pool as normal-priority work. Put new, unreviewed vault-only maps in a separate lowest-priority exploration pool, with clear unaudited/missing-data indicators. Activate this pool after exact identity and safe previews are available. Group map families across acquisition, annotation and train/test splits so variants do not dominate the dataset.

Acquisition and audit sequence

  1. Produce a dry-run manifest of missing parent/version identities, URLs, known sizes and disk-budget estimate. Reconcile the existing local library and already imported vault representatives; never download known files twice.
  2. Agree the batch size and storage location before the full backfill. Resume sequential bounded batches, respect server retry guidance, and cache results. Record timestamps, hashes, source IDs, failures and retry state.
  3. Validate archives before extraction: reject traversal, absolute paths, symlinks escaping the destination and unreasonable decompression sizes. Parse map/Lua data without executing untrusted scripts.
  4. Run the existing technical audit and explicit regression checks for Astro water and Fast Rush water, including exact-version joins, height/water levels, coordinate transforms, passability and buildability. Missing is not zero.
  5. Extract reclaim position and resource yield per prop, spawn/marker positions, terrain and water masks. Publish versioned derived products with audit flags.
  6. Attach overlays only when aligned to the vault preview and validated against known examples. Keep vault imagery as the base and label overlays as extracted evidence. Each overlay can be switched off and its provenance inspected.

Symmetry and team positioning are separate properties

Record separately:

  • Terrain/resource symmetry: reflection axes, 180-degree rotation, higher-order rotation, approximate symmetry, or none/unknown. Do not assume every competitive map has a reflection axis; rotation and gameplay balance need separate checks.
  • Spawn symmetry: whether the active start positions match under those transforms.
  • Team orientation for a selected configuration: left/right, top/bottom, the two diagonal pairings, interleaved, radial/FFA, custom or unknown.
  • Team grouping: clustered teammates, distributed starts/lanes, interleaved teams; within-team and cross-team distances, with scale-normalized measures.
  • Positional specialization and teamwork dependence remain human assessments; geometry can suggest but cannot establish either one.

Use normalized map coordinates and explicitly document origin and preview rotation. Infer candidate transforms from terrain and resource/spawn positions; report tolerance/error and confidence instead of asserting exact symmetry. Team assignments require active spawn selection and team membership, not just all capacity slots. Adaptive spawn/resource settings can change the answer.

Use the owner's examples as configuration-specific review fixtures: Seton's bottom-left versus top-right, Gap left versus right, Canis River top versus bottom. Verify slot assignments and coordinate conventions on exact versions.

After audited overlays exist, add selectable active slots/team colors and ask: 'How are the teams arranged in the setup you are assessing?' and 'How grouped are teammates at the start?' Show inferred suggestions separately from player answers, allow corrections, and store the chosen configuration, slots, team assignments, inference version and confidence. Keep geometry annotations distinct from subjective gameplay quality and do not retrofit old reviews with guesses.
