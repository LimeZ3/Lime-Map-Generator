The larger run's best held-out denoising loss is 17.3% lower. This is not a 17.3% improvement in gameplay quality. Architecture, regularization and compute budget differ, so this does not isolate parameter count as the cause.

The multiscale checkpoint selected by validation is step 36,509, at 4.89 h. The final checkpoint scored 0.010175, about 5.1% worse than the best. This is a reason to keep the best checkpoint, not proof of memorization by itself.

Fresh Evaluation

Best EMA checkpoints from both runs were sampled at identical seeds, requests, 1v1 base positions, 100 DDIM steps and CFG 1.5. Both use the same deterministic rotation-symmetry/water-preserving/light-smoothing decoder, without resource pads or passage carving. Each model produced 12 water-sweep maps and 32 control maps: 88 samples total. Raw and final geometry were measured separately.

An evaluation-only CUDA allocation error interrupted the first sampling process. The saved samples were verified and only the missing 22 were generated in a fresh process with per-sample CUDA-cache cleanup. Training/checkpoints were unaffected. The generation timing in generation.json covers the resumed invocation only, not all earlier sampling work.

Findings

  • Visually, the multiscale samples have smoother, broader terrain formations and less fine noisy relief. This is a visual judgment, not a measured quality score.
  • Water fraction is reasonably steerable, not exact. Mean absolute coverage error across requests of 5%, 20% and 45%: small 2.23 percentage points, multiscale 2.18 percentage points. Maximum raw/final area drift is below 0.12 points.
  • In the four-seed front-width comparison, the requested wider front gives a wider final front in 4/4 small and 3/4 multiscale cases. Mean measured changes are +46.8 and +30.6 map units, respectively. These are modest effects, not accurate reproduction of the requested absolute widths.
  • Fragmentation moves in the expected direction in 4/4 small cases and 3/3 comparable multiscale cases. The fourth multiscale comparison is invalid because a supposedly larger-span terrain has disconnected bases; it is not a success. This fragmentation metric is not a count of independent military lanes.
  • Bottleneck contraction has mixed, generally small effects. Stronger/longer bottlenecks are not reliably controlled; requested sustained restriction duration increases in only 1/4 cases for each model, and the multiscale mean change is in the wrong direction.
  • Disconnection requests fail in all four seeds for both models, before and after postprocessing: the requested-disconnected bases remain connected.
  • Neither model triggers the coarse copy-warning heuristic in its 12 water samples. The eight-orientation nearest-training-map comparison is not proof against memorization, local reuse or near-copies under other transformations.

These are exploratory four-seed comparisons, not statistical guarantees. The larger model is a promising visual terrain baseline, but has not demonstrated better strategic steering. More parameters and hours did not fix every control. The next work should target conditioning/data support and gameplay-level checks, rather than merely extending the same training run.
