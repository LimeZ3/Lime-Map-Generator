Joint Terrain Results: 23 October 2026

The longer terrain-only experiment completed all 12,000 updates. Height, water boundary and depth were learned simultaneously by the same network. Resources, props and textures did not train in this experiment.

Run

  • Run: data/models/terrain_joint_10km_1v1_long_v1.
  • Training/validation: 1,688/170 map versions, preserving the source family split.
  • Model: 2,986,739 parameters, bottleneck attention, EMA, bfloat16 CUDA training.
  • Batch: 16. Budget: 192,000 sampled map presentations, 12 times the previous terrain smoke run's presentation count.
  • Duration: 1,039 seconds (17.3 minutes), including periodic validation/previews but excluding data-loading startup.
  • Peak allocated GPU memory: 1,862 MiB on the RTX 5080.
  • EMA validation objective: 0.85116 initially, 0.01645 finally. The best checkpoint was at update 12,000. This loss is not comparable numerically to the old encoding.
  • Six new focused unit tests passed. Encoding audit passed all 2,077 prepared versions, with physical round-trip errors below 0.0001 world units.
  • The test split was prepared/audited but never used for model fitting/selection.

Fresh-Noise Generation

The final sweep uses four seeds and three water settings, with no source encoder. The following measurements use unmirrored outputs before any geometric cleanup:

Requested Water Mean Predicted Water Height/Water Agreement
0% 0.2% 99.9%
20% 20.9% 98.4%
40% 43.1% 98.2%

Water amount increased monotonically across the three settings for all four tested seeds. These are preliminary conditional-control results, not exact area guarantees. Dry requests can still produce a small pond; wet requests can overshoot.

The two common seeds allow a checkpoint comparison without changing the sample cohort. At 2,000 updates, their 40%-water outputs averaged 14 water components, 5.5 tiny components, and 93.0% height/water agreement. At 10,000 updates they averaged 6.5 components, one tiny component, and 97.7% agreement. Exact body counts are not a gameplay-quality score, but the water fields have become more coherent.

Both the representation and training budget changed, so this does not isolate a causal benefit from extra compute alone. Height remains fairly soft and some details resemble generic rolling terrain; this is not a final competitive style.

Symmetry And Geometry

The current inspection pipeline averages a map with its 180-degree rotation. Averaging signed shoreline distances can erase non-overlapping water bodies: the 20%-water final sweep shrank from 20.9% raw water to 10.7% after this step. This is a postprocessing problem and must not be misreported as failed raw water-area conditioning.

Before water/height reconciliation, 11 of 12 mirrored height fields had a structural land connection between the requested base anchors. After the separate joint projection, 12 of 12 did. The projection changed model-grid heights by 0.69 world units on average. These are approximate structural checks, not actual game-engine validation. Requested base anchors are inputs, not learned spawn placements.

The next terrain task is symmetry handling that preserves learned water amount, followed by finer base/shoreline checks and optional higher-resolution refinement. Playable export and resource placement remain separate milestones.

Held-Out Diagnostics

One-pass denoising was evaluated on all 170 validation versions. At diffusion timestep 100, wet-map water IoU averaged 0.937; mean height error was 0.98 world units. At timestep 500, IoU averaged 0.819 and mean height error was 2.41 units. These reconstructions start from noised source tensors. They are not fresh maps and are saved separately from the generation galleries.

A coarse, rotation/reflection-aware nearest-training-source comparison is saved for the final fresh samples. It is an inspection aid, not proof of originality or exhaustive clone detection.
