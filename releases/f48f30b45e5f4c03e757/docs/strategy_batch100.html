Strategy labels: 100-map audit milestone

22 October 2026. The user approved starting/rear economy and authorized a timed 100-map batch before deciding whether to process the full library.

Reproducible input

  • Manifest: data/manifests/strategy_labels_batch100_20261022.json, seed 20261022.
  • Available local catalogue entries only: no network requests, new downloads, replays or retries of the historical failed map downloads.
  • Nine familiar controls, then a proportional size-stratified reference sample up to 80 maps including those controls; 10 further hidden entries and 10 other visible entries. One control is hidden and two are outside the reference: the actual totals are 11 hidden, 89 visible, 78 reference members.
  • Sizes: 4 at 256², 56 at 512², 37 at 1024², 2 at 2048², 1 at 4096².
  • Family grouping joins same parent ID, normalized name or identical terrain bytes before a deterministic 80/10/10 family split. Actual map counts are 78 train, 9 validation, 13 test. These heuristics are conservative evidence, not exhaustive visual duplicate detection across the whole library.

This deliberately includes familiar and difficult cases; it is a throughput and correctness pilot, not an unbiased popularity-weighted vault survey.

Operating policy

Pipeline 1.2.1, configuration configs/strategy_labels_v1.json, two workers. Run: data/processed/strategy_labels_batch100/v1.2.1/6826369ec95be771.

The frozen label specification defines the fixed balanced resource weight, automatic 5% saturation, medium drop gates, automatic route strength and base strength 1. It preserves the approved route/drop models, 1s/entity and three reclaim tiers, and mass-only valuation. The three UEF naval representatives are frigate, destroyer and cruiser missiles.

Each completed version gets an independent audit before it counts as scored: sealed hashes, physical paths, no unwanted crossings, symmetry, resource allocation, deduplicated opportunity shares, full spatial opening ledger and naval basin/range witnesses. Per-map audits are saved beside the result. Unsupported inputs stay explicit exclusions or failures with null ratings.

Relative economy, openness and control boundaries are fit once to successful, resolved training-reference entries in this trial, with IDs and cut points saved in calibration.json. Future batches must reuse that calibration explicitly or declare a new release. Opportunity ratings use the fixed share bands.

Input/audit repair during the pilot

The first 1.2.0 attempt was stopped after the audit exposed named playable-area disagreements. Its artifacts remain at v1.2.0/28e3943ab4cf556d; they are not mixed into the final result. Both worker leases were released before restarting.

The 1.2.1 patch does not change route, reclaim, drop or rating rules:

  • Validate save rectangles using the existing terrain reader's full-map fallback for zero-sized placeholder areas. Adaptive Silvia triggered this case.
  • Resolve the supported literal, instantaneous start-expanded default instead of retaining a cropped AREA_1. Adaptive Neptune starts in AREA_4 under its declared default.
  • Reject complex timed boundary code as unsupported rather than treat a moving playable area as a static full map. Adaptive Aria Expanding is such a case.
  • Compare cached independent-reader vertices/types under the current resolved scope. The older C# adapter sometimes chose the first area in the save file, which was AREA_2 or AREA_3; equality of source terrain alone did not make its already-scoped navigation grid a valid comparison.

The current navigation, resource inventory, front fitting and opening benchmark all use the same resolved bounds. The original save scope remains in the raw extraction record as source metadata; the resolved scientific scope is recorded explicitly in the pipeline result.

Completed result

100/100 attempted: 88 scored, 8 excluded, 4 failed with explicit input reasons. No map was substituted for an unsupported case. The twelve unrated cases are listed in the report/CSV: three lack an unambiguous mirrored opponent, five have unsupported resource scripts/tables, two have unresolved/timed playable bounds, and two have no active mexes. They receive no synthetic zero ratings.

  • Processing and independent per-map audits: 42m 53s with two workers.
  • Preview/rating export: 33s. Total clean batch: 43m 25s.
  • Median successful map: 26.1s; 90th percentile: 97.2s.
  • Slowest: Diezle Sundered, 13m 58s, with independent fits on both sides.
  • The earlier interrupted 1.2.0 probe additionally used 7.6 minutes before the input-scope patch; that is separate from clean-run throughput.
  • 1,152 front configurations, 5,585 front-economy events and 7,260 spatial opening events passed audit across the 88 scored maps. Independent-reader vertices/types were available for 74; 14 had no such cache to compare.
  • 176 primary PNGs passed integrity and native-dimension checks. All 100 CSV rows match the frozen JSON ratings, validity and confidence; location returns conserve the total early mass. The flat CSV has 96 columns.
  • Thirteen maps have a known-reclaim lower bound and reduced confidence. No front-count search was censored at K8. All 88 scored maps have all rating axes.
  • Relative calibration uses 55 training-reference maps for mex supply and 44 resolved training-reference maps for the other four relative axes. Eligible IDs and numeric boundaries are saved, not refit per viewing/filter.

Outputs, relative to the completed run directory:

  • index.html: sortable rating table, all outcomes, combined strategy and starting/rear previews, links to detailed inspection.
  • labels.csv / labels.json: raw metrics, twelve independent ratings 1–5, confidence/validity, identity and family split.
  • scoring_report.md: quantitative familiar-map comparisons, high/low examples, exclusions and interpretation limits.
  • validation.json, label_validation.json, release_manifest.json: stored audit results, export checks and release hashes.
  • calibration.json, run.json, code_snapshot/: saved thresholds, inputs, configuration, source/code hashes and a verified snapshot of 66 scientific files plus report sources/runtime versions.

Two interpretation limits deserve review before extending coverage. Naval range potential can overrate steep coasts: Astro has no frigate targets, but destroyer and cruiser radius masks reach raised land without projectile obstruction tests. The openness scalar measures an approach bottleneck; Tabula Rasa's 11.2-unit minimum does not establish that its entire frontline is closed. Preserve both these raw measurements and their definitions; no thresholds were tuned to force the familiar examples into desired ratings.

The full-library run remains unstarted pending review of this batch.
