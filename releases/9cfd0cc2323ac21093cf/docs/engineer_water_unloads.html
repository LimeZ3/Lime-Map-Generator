Engineer water-unload correction

15 October 2026. Current viewer: http://127.0.0.1:5177/drop_response_strength_1v1/index.html

Engineers now use the hover navigation layer and may unload on either land or water. The old amphibious engineer model and dry-shore landing requirement were incorrect for this calculation. Hover movement ignores seabed slope and the amphibious 25-unit depth limit; those existing amphibious-unit masks remain unchanged for their own uses.

Implementation

  • strategy/opportunities.py builds separate dry ground-payload and hover-engineer unload masks and candidate points.
  • Engineer walking, post-unload travel, both sides' expansion arrival races and territory access use the hover graph.
  • Water resource areas group and measure local travel through hover connectivity. Land/island resource grouping remains as before. For a land target, engineer landing access is calculated separately from the dry land-payload field, allowing an engineer to unload in adjacent water and then move onto accessible shore.
  • Ground attack payloads still use dry unload points and land movement. Static AA placements also remain dry.
  • engineer_landing is exported alongside the original dry landing mask in both baseline and strength-scenario NPZ files.
  • Resources expose site_surface; regions and score rows expose water_mexes; raw rows identify engineer_navigation_layer=hover and expansion_landing_surface=water|land.
  • Summary JSON/CSV and the viewer report underwater mex counts. Area details show the selected unload surface.

The footprint remains a static feasibility approximation: a 7x7 open patch in the payload's movement layer, sampled in 8x8 tiles. Engine collision and detailed transport unload behavior are not simulated. No attack-route geometry, economic threshold, route/base strength or naval reach setting was changed.

The strict/medium resource-count floors still reject these isolated single-mex areas. That is an economic screen, not a water-access exclusion. Eligibility also depends on side, distance, resource value and response checks. Faster hover walking can reduce transport value on some land/island targets, which is why the corrected model may remove a previous expansion flag while adding offshore flags.

The full-strength control now has 14 underwater mexes qualifying in the permissive preset as well. Astro and the selected original Seton's version have no underwater resource areas in these inputs; their attack predictions remain unchanged. All three map outputs, nine response-strength scenarios, PNG overlays and CSV exports were refreshed.

Validation

All 52 targeted tests passed. The new regression places two mexes far offshore, beyond any dry shore, with the amphibious mask blocked and the hover mask open. It verifies finite hover travel, a direct water unload, a valid medium expansion opportunity and no land-payload unload at that water target.

Actual-map checks verify every engineer unload against the hover footprint and every attack unload against the dry footprint; water-mex summary totals agree with scored areas. Existing checks still pass for candidate nesting, response-strength monotonicity, conserved resource/destination membership, source hashes and PNG/numerical-layer alignment.
